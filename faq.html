Life Transitions Counseling and Therapy

Life Transitions Counseling

Life Transitions counseling is designed for seasons when something important and fundamental is shifting. Whether planned or unexpected, it can be some of the most challenging moments we face. Sometimes, a change feels like a heavy weight that is hard to shake. Other times, change feels like an invitation to step into something bigger and more meaningful.

I provide specialized clinical support for:

  • Professional Transitions: Navigating career pivots, burnout recovery, or retirement.
  • Relationship & Family Transitions: Adjusting to major changes in partnerships, caregiving, or family dynamics.
  • Life Stage & Identity Transitions: Adjusting to young adulthood, mid-life realignment, or relocation.

My Clinical Approach

My practice is built on the integration of clinical psychotherapy and specialized Transition Intelligence (TQ) framework. Transitions change more than your external circumstances; they reshape your identity, your routines, and your sense of direction.

I help you move from uncertainty to clarity by addressing both the emotional and psychological aspects of change. My goal is to help you navigate the "in-between" with clinical steadiness, intention, and a clear sense of purpose.

How does Transitional Intelligence (TQ) help with life transitions and change? What are the benefits?

Life is a series of transitions, and having a framework makes the process feel less disorienting. Transitional Intelligence, or TQ, gives you the language and a structure to help you understand how change works from the inside out. It is the process of developing your internal capacity to move through significant transitions with more awareness and less reactivity.

When life changes, it's common to rush for answers or power through to the next thing. But transition unfold in stages and have a natural rhythm. By recognizing these stages, you can move through them with more intention. These skills are a lifelong investment, allowing you to navigate every future shift with greater self awareness and confidence throughout your entire life. 

In our work together, transitional intelligence helps you:

  • Understand the stages of transition so you don’t feel lost in the process
  • Name what feels stuck or confusing
  • Build practical tools for coping and decision-making
  • Develop more confidence while navigating uncertain seasons
  • Use change as a pathway for personal growth

What is your experience working with high-achievers and high performing professionals, and how does that influence your therapy approach?

My professional background as a psychotherapist includes over 25 years of experience across hospitals, community mental health, and high-performance environments—including Start Ups, Big Tech, Fortune 500 companies, and global universities. Having worked both within these high-achieving, high-pressure cultures and as a provider for clients navigating them, I understand the unique rigor they demand. This combination of clinical expertise and professional experience is essential for high-performers for three reasons:

  • I speak your language: You do not need to spend our sessions explaining organizational culture, the pace of high-stakes environments, or the pressure to perform. I understand these dynamics, which allows us to focus immediately on the deeper issues: burnout, imposter syndrome, leadership stress, and the struggle to disconnect.
  • I identify patterns quickly: I help you see how your environment—workload, ambition, and career demands—is shaping your internal state. We move past surface-level symptoms to address the systemic patterns contributing to your current sense of "stuckness."
  • Clinical rigor meets strategic growth: We make sense of those pressures together, moving toward clarity and personal growth that is relevant, realistic, and sustainable. We can focus on what matters most: helping you reconnect with your values, your well-being, and your next purposeful step.

What is your experience with retirement transitions?

Retirement is one of the most significant identity transitions we face. It is far more than a financial milestone; it’s a profound shift in your daily rhythm, relationships, and sense of purpose.

During my time at Harvard University, I developed a curriculum and support group specifically for faculty and staff navigating early retirement. My approach is uniquely informed by my expertise in geriatric psychiatry, which provides a deep understanding of the emotional and developmental shifts of later-life transitions. I use the MEA (Modern Elder Academy) framework to help you reimagine meaning, and discover how you want to share your wisdom and continue to build your legacy.

Whether your retirement is long-awaited or feels unexpected and disorienting, we work together to cultivate a chapter that feels intentional and connected to who you are now—not just what you used to do.

How do I know if I need counseling for life transitions, and what is your approach?

Many of my clients come to me not because they are in a state of crisis, but because they have reached a "threshold." They are high-performing individuals who feel that despite their external success, they are disconnected from their own values.

You may benefit from counseling for life transitions if you find yourself experiencing:

  • Misalignment: A persistent feeling of stuckness that your life looks successful on paper but no longer fits who you are or where you want to go.
  • Systemic Burnout: Professional exhaustion that is spilling over into your personal life, affecting your ability to disconnect or regulate your stress.
  • Communication Breakdown: Feeling as though you are repeating the same unproductive patterns in your professional leadership or personal relationships.

My work is designed for those who want more than talk therapy. I integrate clinical expertise with strategic, transition-focused frameworks to help you navigate periods of significant change.

We move beyond surface-level symptoms to address the systemic patterns—at work and in your personal life—that contribute to your current plateau. My sessions are structured, forward-looking, and focused on helping you reconnect with your values and well-being. This is a space for individuals who want clinical safety combined with a practical, goal-oriented roadmap for their next chapter.

What is the difference between counseling and therapy?

The terms "counseling" and "therapy" are often used to describe the same professional service. Both are conducted by licensed mental health providers, follow strict ethical standards, and are designed to improve your mental and emotional well-being.

While the terms are often used interchangeably, some people distinguish them based on the focus of the work:

  • Counseling: This term is often used to describe a more goal-oriented, shorter-term partnership. It is frequently applied to situations where we are navigating a specific life event—such as a career pivot, a transition in family roles, or a period of high stress—where the focus is on developing specific strategies and actionable skills.
  • Therapy: This term often implies a deeper, more comprehensive approach to your emotional landscape. Therapy frequently involves exploring underlying patterns, processing past experiences, and addressing systemic issues such as long-term anxiety, depression, or complex trauma. It is often a longer-term process focused on healing and broad psychological growth.

In my practice, I offer both.

Because I am a Licensed Independent Clinical Social Worker (LICSW), I am trained to provide both specialized, goal-oriented transition work and comprehensive clinical therapy. My goal is to meet you where you are. Whether you need a roadmap to navigate a specific life change or a steady, supportive space to work through long-term emotional patterns, we will tailor our sessions to the needs of your current season.

Do you accept insurance for counseling?

I operate as a private-pay practice and I do not bill insurance companies directly. Each session is $250.00. This allows us to prioritize your care, goals, and privacy without the interference or limitations of insurance-mandated protocols.

Working outside of the insurance model offers several key benefits for your treatment:

  • No mental health diagnosis is required to start. In a private-pay model, a diagnosis is not required, which protects your privacy and ensures your permanent medical record reflects only what is strictly necessary.
  • We are not limited by session frequency or treatment duration, allowing us to build a plan that is truly tailored to your needs and pace.
  • Your treatment information remains confidential with no third-party access.
  • Payment is simple, transparent, and predictable

If you have a PPO plan with out-of-network mental health benefits, you might be able to get reimbursed for sessions. I can provide a superbill (a detailed receipt) for you to submit to your insurance company or FSA/HSA.

If you choose to submit a superbill, please keep in mind:

  • A mental health diagnosis is required
  • Insurance may request access to your treatment records
  • Reimbursement isn’t guaranteed

Before starting, it can be helpful to call your insurance provider and ask:

  • Do I have out-of-network mental health benefits?
  • What percentage do they cover?
  • What’s my deductible, and how much have I met?
  • How do I submit claims?
  • What are the rates for CPT codes 90791 ( initial evaluation) and 90834 (45 minutes)?
  • Is pre-authorization needed?

Good Faith Estimate: By law, if you choose not to use your health insurance and opt to pay for therapy services out of pocket, you are entitled to a Good Faith Estimate of medical costs under the No Surprises Act. Click here for more information.
